# Evidence-based study: Is there a need for an EMS in Kenya?

> **Status:** Draft for user review. Built from the evidence register
> (highest IDs D-074, C-035, I-012) and the accepted Phase 6 verdict.
> Prepared November 2026.

**Sources.** AR = EPRA, *Energy & Petroleum Statistics Report for the Year
Ended 30th June 2026*. BR = EPRA, *Biannual Energy & Petroleum Statistics
Report, Financial Year 2025/2026* (Jul-Dec 2025). Printed page = PDF page in
both.
**Labels.** DATA = reported. CALCULATION = derived from reported values
(working in the evidence register). INTERPRETATION = judgment. Outside
information is marked "external, unverified".
**Periods.** FY-H1 = Jul-Dec 2025. FY-H2 = Jan-Jun 2026 (AR minus BR, a
CALCULATION).

## 1. Executive summary

**Question.** Do two EPRA reports (annual FY2025/26; biannual Jul-Dec 2025)
show a real, evidence-backed need for an Energy Management System (EMS) in
Kenya, and at what level?

**Verdict: Inconclusive.** Confidence is Medium that this is the right label
on these reports, and Low in any statement about the need itself.

**Key reasons**

- The reports measure large problems: losses of 21.38% against a 16.5%
  allowance, and average monthly SAIDI of 13.16 hours against a 1.5-hour
  target. They do not show that monitoring, forecasting, optimisation or
  control would fix them. Loss causes are reported as physical and
  commercial. Reliability causes are not reported.
- No item scores High. Six score Medium, each missing a link: size, cause,
  hourly data or an adoption barrier.
- Captive solar grew 72.8 MW, about half of national capacity additions, with
  no output or export data. That is a gap in published data, not proof of
  unmet need.
- The reports cannot show whether EMS-type tools already exist, so "need
  already met" cannot be tested.
- Petroleum, LPG, pass-through costs, household and e-mobility show no
  EMS-addressable need on this evidence.

**Consequence.** No EMS type or level is recommended. The next steps are data
requests, a scan of existing tools and a data-quality note for EPRA. Any of
these could move the verdict either way.

### 2.2 Limitations

1. **Mixed bases (DATA).** AR mixes financial-year and calendar-year series.
   BR figure labels "H1/H2" are calendar halves, so "H2 2025" means
   Jul-Dec 2025 (BR Figs 3.3, 3.5, 3.7, 5.12).
2. **Not independent (INTERPRETATION, I-001).** Agreement between AR and BR
   for Jul-Dec 2025 is not a second confirmation. A differing figure is the
   useful signal.
3. **Undefined terms.** "Effective capacity" is not defined. "Consumption",
   "utilisation" and "sales" are used interchangeably. Reliability-index
   treatment of planned outages and major events is not stated. The loss-rate
   formula is not given.
4. **Unresolved conflicts between or within reports.** These lower confidence
   in some sizes: loss rates not reproducible from reported volumes (C-008);
   implied FY-H2 sales jump (C-009); CAIDI "complied" text against Fig 2.20
   (D-069); imports and thermal text against figures (C-012, D-066, D-046);
   off-grid capacity 47.58 against 46.24 MW (D-006, D-007); distribution
   growth baseline (C-011); Coast share (C-016); solar technician licence rows
   (D-053 against D-054); pump prices that differ between reports (C-026).
5. **Silence is not absence.** Blanks and omissions are data gaps. The reports
   are statistical summaries, so they would not necessarily name systems or
   records that exist (I-005).
6. **Time.** Nothing after 30 June 2026. One year of detailed data.
7. **Judgment.** Relevance scores and hypothesis confidences are analytical
   judgments, not measurements.

**INTERPRETATION.**

1. No item reaches High. Each Medium has a named missing link: loss split and
   location; outage cause; hourly data and contract terms; captive output and
   exports; ToU eligible population and barrier; audit implementation.
2. The largest measured problems (losses, peak margin) have reported causes
   that are physical, commercial or capacity-related. Reliability has no
   reported cause.
3. Where the mechanism fits best (curtailment, captive solar, ToU, audits),
   the reports give the least size evidence.
4. Programme data exist for C&I-linked instruments (ToU, audits, captive
   capacity) and none for feeder or hourly behaviour. More rows at a level
   reflects what the reports measure, not necessarily greater need (I-011).

## 5. Hypothesis testing

**"Significant" (for H1) requires three things:** (i) a measured magnitude in
the reports; (ii) a stated, plausible causal link to monitoring, forecasting,
optimisation or control; (iii) no evidence the need is already met. Missing
data is listed separately and is not counted against any hypothesis. H0 and H1
are mutually exclusive; the others can hold together.

**Evidence-handling notes (INTERPRETATION).**

- "Curtailment rose every month Feb to Jun" is weak evidence of worsening.
  FY-H2 curtailment is flat year on year, the June share (3.65%) is below Jul-25
  (4.90%) and Aug-25 (3.76%), and no prior-year monthly series exists (I-008).
- "Curtailment and ToU improved with no EMS reported" is not evidence against
  need. The reports are silent on any EMS contribution (I-009).
- Agreement between AR and BR for Jul-Dec 2025 is not independent (I-001).

## 6. Verdict and confidence

**Verdict: Inconclusive.**

The two reports show large, measured problems. They do not show that any of
them is one that monitoring, forecasting, optimisation or control would
address. They also cannot show whether such capability already exists (I-005).
Absence of data is a data gap, not evidence against need.
